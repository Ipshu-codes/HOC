Deity / tradition Yogambar Jnaneshwari, the principal seat (mool peeth) of the Ashtamatrikas, in the Vajrayana Buddhist tradition; worshipped by Buddhists and Hindus, and the lineage deity (kul devata) of many Bajracharya and Shakya families of Patan. Festivals Charhe (Krishna Paksha Chaturdashi) The yearly jatra (Chaitra) View on 3D Map → History The temple's main deity is Yogambar Jnaneshwari , but most people know the temple simply as Mhepi Ajima . The daily worship is carried on by the temple's chief priest, Bhushan Ratna Bajracharya, as his father and grandfather did before him. According to the priest, Mhepi lies to the north among the Ashtamatrikas, the eight mother goddesses around the city. He cites a vamshavali (chronicle) which records that the Ashtamatrikas were established in the shape of a sword (khadga) to protect Kantipur, today's Kathmandu. He adds that the history of the place is older still: the seat (peeth) of Yogambar Jnaneshwari is said to be based on the Yogambar tantra of the Vajrayana Buddhist tradition. No historical documents about the temple are known so far, although researchers and interested people have written articles about it. How the name came about In the temple's story (see Legends & Beliefs), the body of a holy man's son was buried here and the peeth was built above it. In Nepal Bhasa mha means body, so a seat built over a body was called Mhasa peeth , a name that in time became Mhepi . What the inscriptions record Nepal Sambat 1120, Pohelathwa 12 (VS 2056, Magh 4) : Shri Amrit Man Shrestha and Prithvi Kumari Shrestha, residents of Mhepi, offered a two-tiered brass roof to Shri Shri Shri Yogambar Jnaneshwari Mhepi Ajima in memory of their ancestors, on the auspicious day of their Janku ceremony. Nepal Sambat 1120, Gunlathwa Navami, Tuesday (VS 2057, Shrawan) : in memory of the late Mathuradas Shrestha and the late Vishnu Devi Shrestha, the family of Rajendra Das Shrestha laid white stone (marble) in the temple of Shri Yogambar Jnaneshwari. Nepal Sambat 1121, Thinla Dwadashi : in memory of the late Gyanmaya Shakya, Buddharatna Shakya and Gyandevi Shakya, Shri Chaitya Ratna Shakya and Sanu Chhori Shakya offered metal-sheet work for the middle part of the main temple of Shri Yogambar Mhepi Ajima. Nepal Sambat 1134, Thilla Dwadashi (VS 2070, Mangsir 29) : Tirtharam Timila and Manik Laxmi Timila of Balaju offered metal-sheet work on both sides of Mhepi Ajima. The bhajan house : a bhajan house was built in the Mhepi temple complex in VS 2029 . When it fell into disrepair and rainwater began to disturb the daily bhajan-kirtan, the bhajan mandal decided on VS 2068 Kartik 30 to rebuild it with the support of devotees. The renovation was completed under the bhajan mandal's chair, Shri Ashtalal Maharjan; the commemorative plaque is dated VS 2069 Jestha 6 . Legends & Beliefs The priest tells the temple's story through the Swayambhu Purana: Manjushri and the lake. The Kathmandu Valley was once a lake, then called Nagdaha. Manjushri came from China to see the jyotirupa , a light on a lotus flower, cut the hill at Chobhar with his sword to drain the water, and made the valley fit to live in. Shantashri. Hearing Kashyap Buddha preach about the light, King Prachanda Dev of Gaud left his kingdom, came to Nepal, took ordination from a Bajracharya guru and, as the monk Shantashri , cared for the light at Swayambhu. Fearing it could be destroyed, he covered it with a stupa. He worshipped it every day with the tantra of Yogambar Jnaneshwari, living at Shantipur, on the north side of Swayambhu. The son given to the goddess. During his practice the goddess asked him for what he loved most, and he gave his only son. She said the boy was a form of a Bodhisattva: his body was to be laid in one place, while she took his soul. The soul was later born as the 555th son of King Yaksharaj Shashi and Queen Maya, and came to Nepal as Rato Machhindranath . The boy's body was buried here, and the peeth was established above it. Because of this link, when the Rato Machhindranath of Bungamati has to be rebuilt, its priests (panju) come here, perform puja and take soil from the temple. The priest remembers them coming after the earthquake of VS 1990, in VS 2054, 2056 and 2066, and, after the earthquake of VS 2072, in VS 2078. The ground is held so sacred that an image of a god is said to be complete only when soil from here is mixed in. Buddhists and Hindus both worship here, and lamas of the Mahayana tradition from Tibet worship the goddess as a yogini. Because of her part in bringing Rato Machhindranath to Nepal, many Bajracharya, Shakya and other families of Patan worship her as their kul devata (lineage deity), more so than families in Kathmandu. The temple bhajan, displayed on a marble plaque by the Mhepi Conservation and Management Committee (dated 2078/5/6, Gunla Punhi), invokes the goddess by several names: जय योगाम्बर जय ज्ञानेश्वरी जय गुह्येश्वरी आमा जय जय अम्बे, जय जय काली जय भुवनेश्वरी श्यामा जय योगाम्बर जय ज्ञानेश्वरी जय म्हयपी आमा जय जय अम्बे, जय जय काली जय भुवनेश्वरी श्यामा Festivals & Jatras Charhe. The main puja is held on Krishna Paksha Chaturdashi, the fourteenth day of the dark fortnight, which is called Charhe. The yearly jatra. The temple itself has no images, only stones (shila) and the peeth, consecrated through tantric practice. For the yearly worship, images were made later; they are kept as the Ashtamatrika goddesses in the god-house (dyochhen) at Tanlachhi . On Chaitra Shukla Chaturdashi the images are brought from Tanlachhi on a khat (palanquin) with due ritual, installed at the temple and worshipped through the night, and a mela is held at the temple. On the full moon the images stay at the temple for devotees. For about 15 years the Mhepi Conservation and Management Committee has held a programme that day and distributed prasad, to about 4,000–5,000 people judging by the prasad given out. The Metropolitan City, the ward and devotees help with the costs. The images are then taken back to Tanlachhi. The next day they are carried around Asan, Indrachowk, Thahiti, Paknajol and Kshetrapati, and the day after they are returned to the god-house with puja. The guthis. The procession lets households worship the goddess at their own doors. Guthis (community trusts) shared the work: one carried the khat, one performed the puja, one played the music. Many of the old members have died and their families have moved away, so the jatra is quieter than it was, but the procession still goes on. Architecture The temple stands on a small wooded hill (thumko), a patch of forest in the middle of the city. At its centre is Jnaneshwari, the principal seat of the Ashtamatrikas, in the form of stones and the peeth rather than an image. The grounds also hold Mahadev, Vishnu, Krishna and the Buddha. According to the priest, most of these images were placed before VS 2030; an inscription dates the Saraswati image to around VS 2005. Below the hill, on the Gunal side, a Narayan temple was established on an empty site about three years ago (as of 2026), and devotees have placed a small Hanuman image. The donor inscriptions (above) record a two-tiered brass roof and metal-sheet work offered to the temple. Present Day The temple is looked after by the Mhepi Conservation and Management Committee (म्हेयपि संरक्षण तथा व्यवस्थापन समिति). Sattals (rest houses) in the temple grounds can be booked in advance through the committee for weddings, bratabandha, puja, feasts and picnics. A bhajan mandal holds bhajan-kirtan in the bhajan house. The grounds include the Shantikar Dhyanagar Dhyanashram (शान्तिकर ध्यानागार ध्यानाश्रम), a meditation hall. The committee asks that no stalls be set up in front of the temple door. The Mhepi Conservation and Management Committee was founded in VS 2054 (1997/98 AD) . It looks after the old images, the daily puja, the forest on the hill, security and cleaning, and runs on donations. The daily puja starts at 4:00 am and ends by 5:30 am , earlier than at many temples, so that devotees coming for darshan are not disturbed. The priest notes that, although the temple appears on online maps, few people know its history or what the name Mhepi means. How to Visit Where: on a small wooded hill in Naya Bazar, KMC Ward No. 16. See it on the 3D map. Daily puja: 4:00–5:30 am. Who may come: the temple is open to people of every faith. Please do not set up stalls in front of the temple door.
