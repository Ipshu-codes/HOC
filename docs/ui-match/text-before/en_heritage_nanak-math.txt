Opening hours 7:00 am – 6:00 pm View on 3D Map → Name and Location According to the math, it has existed since ancient times and has always been run by renunciant mahants of the Nepali guru–disciple (guru–shishya) tradition, a tradition that continues today. For this reason the math's tradition has been protected and nurtured, and this tapasthali (place of penance) of sadhus, with its historical, traditional and cultural importance, has remained as it was to this day. In this tradition a guru takes a celibate disciple, teaches him the daily rites of the ashram and the worship of the Vishnu Panchayatan, and trains him in the tradition's spiritual practices. Once the disciple is accomplished, sadhus of many sects, mahants, the head of the Guthi Sansthan, local scholars and devotees are invited and he is declared mahant. Under the math's rules, the outgoing mahant recommends his successor to the Guthi Sansthan, which approves the appointment, and responsibility is handed over with a bhandara for the sadhus, as instructed by Brahmaharidas Maharaj. The line of mahants According to the math, the guru–disciple tradition has come down from Lord Shri Ram, Mother Janaki, Hanumanji, Brahmaji, Sanak, Sanandan, Sanatan, Sanatkumar and Narad, through Brahmaharidas Maharaj, who is in living bhu-samadhi, to this day, and the present, 23rd mahant is Shri Naval Kishor Das Ji Maharaj . An ancient stone image of Nandi still stands here, and the place is called Nandeshwar Mahadev. Tapoban — Because sadhus who practised here — living on water alone (jalahari), on air (pawanahari), on milk (dudhahari) or on fruit (falahari), standing on one leg or holding one arm raised — attained spiritual accomplishment, the math was called Tapoban, the forest of penance. Shankha Ban — Pleased by the penance of the saints, the gods are said to have sounded conch shells (shankha) here at night, giving the name Shankha Ban. Sangat Ban — After daily readings of the Vedas, Puranas, Upanishads, Shruti, Smriti, Ramayana, Bhagavat, Bhagavad Gita, Sant Vani and Bhaktamal, the mahants and visiting saints hold satsang with devotees, so the math is still called Sangat Ban. Mahanta Math — Because it has always been run by mahants, local people know it as the Mahanta Math. The living samadhi — The math regards the three samadhi-siddha saints as still in tapasya, a source of inspiration for all people and a subject for study and research. According to the math, Brahmaharidas Maharaj continues to give guidance on running it. Sadhus from Nepal, India and abroad who come to Kathmandu for darshan of Pashupatinath also visit this samadhi temple. The math's gurus brought the Guru Granth Sahib here for study; before that, and still today, the Vedas, Puranas, Upanishads, Ramayana and Bhagavat were kept, worshipped and read in daily satsang. Special Festivals, Celebrations and Holy Days The math's annual programme: Teej (dar khane din) — tithi of Buddhi Das Maharaj Krishna Ashtami Dhwajarohan — on the full moon when the Sorha Shraddha begin, the flag is changed and a bhandara is held Sharadiya Navaratri — Durga puja and worship of nine girls and one boy Vijaya Dashami — birthday of Nem Muni Maharaj Kojagrat Purnima — tithi of Mangal Das Maharaj Vasant Panchami — tithi of Krishna Das Maharaj Mahashivaratri Vasantik Navaratri Daily worship Puja and readings for the Vishnu Panchayatan deities and the sacred texts, and puja, abhishek, aarti and bhog at the heritage shrines within the math grounds, are performed regularly every morning and evening. Worship, care and protection of the cows; puja at the samadhi temple; abhishek at the Hanuman temple and on the Shivalinga; service to Nandeshwar Mahadev; puja of the Vishnu paduka beneath the peepal tree; water abhishek for Tulsi Maharani; abhishek of the shaligram as a form of Vishnu; and the akhanda dhuni is kept burning without a break. materials where the hall for religious discourses and satsang is under construction Present Day The math is run and managed by its mahant, Shri Naval Kishor Das Ji Maharaj. Daily life includes puja, abhishek, kirtan and bhajan; periodic mahayoga sadhana, katha, pravachan and satsang; morning and evening aarti; and service to cows, guests and visiting saints. The sadhus living here practise mahayoga, contemplating the soul and the Supreme. Paddy, wheat, maize, greens and vegetables grown on the math's land are used to serve visiting saints, cows, brahmins and guests, and for the regular puja, tithis, festivals and bhandaras. The dharmashala, Vishnu Panchayatan temple, sant niwas, gaushala, kitchen (pakshala), pathshala, yogashala and yajnashala, which have stood since ancient times, are in need of reconstruction. How to Visit Where: east of Balaju Chowk, there is a Shivalaya to the left of the Bishnumati bridge over the Bishnumati River. Above it, beside the Bishnumati Corridor, is a signboard reading 'महायोगी श्री ब्रह्महरिदास महाराजको जीवित भू-समाधि स्थल, प्राचीन उदासीन नानक मठ' (Living Bhu-Samadhi Site of Mahayogi Shri Brahmaharidas Maharaj, Prachin Udasin Nanak Math). From there, you can climb up to visit the math's historic heritage. Hours: 7:00 am – 6:00 pm, open to all visitors.
